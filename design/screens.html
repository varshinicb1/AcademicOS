Primitives & states

foundation

The vocabulary every screen is drawn from. Read this first — the rules here are what the rest of the page depends on, and they are the parts most likely to drift.

Status — a dot and a word. No capsule, no background, no border
On pace At risk Overdue by 3 days Scheduled Not started

Colour means one thing: something needs a person. Only at risk and overdue are coloured. Healthy states are grey on purpose — the previous version made ten unrelated things green, which meant a green row told you nothing and a red one was lost among them. The hollow dot distinguishes not started from scheduled without a second colour.

Buttons — every state designed, not defaulted

Minimum height 32px, and 26px for the small variant — both clear the WCAG 2.5.8 floor of 24px. The row action buttons in tables are the ones to watch; they are the smallest targets in the product.

Selection — the control that was hardest to use

in a browser, driven by JS

The box is 17px but the hit target is 34px, and it is a real <input type=checkbox> — a custom-drawn one would break keyboard and screen-reader use. The whole row tints when selected, so the state is legible without hunting for the tick. The bulk bar is attached to the table it acts on rather than floating at the foot of the page.

Three containers that are not status, and do not look like it
Competency-based MCQ Science × 404

A tag labels (neutral, never coloured). A chip is a filter the user can remove, so it carries an exit. A count is a number and keeps tabular alignment. The previous version put all three inside status pills, along with mark values and timestamps, which is why nothing read as anything.

33. Accessibility obligations that change these screens

WCAG 2.2 AA

Three of the new WCAG 2.2 criteria contradict patterns used above, so they are flagged rather than assumed. These are not optional and they are not retrofittable cheaply.

2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA)

Timetable builder (9) and Curriculum sequence (4) are drag-operated above.

Requirement: every drag action needs a single-pointer, non-drag alternative. Both screens already show a "Move to position…" control. Confirm that satisfies your reading — it is the interpretation I am relying on.

Also affects: selecting a holiday date range, and any slider.

2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA)

The sticky topbar and sticky table headers can hide a focused row. Requirement: a focused element must not be entirely hidden by author content.

Affects every long table: Coverage (2), Difficult topics (3), Audit (15), Question bank (17), Schedule (10).

Fix is scroll-margin-top on focusable rows — cheap, but must be written into every table.

2.5.8 Target Size (AA)

Minimum 24 × 24 CSS px for any pointer target. The dense row height is only 32px, so row action buttons are the risk.

Every "Open", "Replace", "Edit" button in a table must either meet 24px or be positioned so its target circle does not intersect a neighbour's.

Buttons now draw at a 32px minimum and 26px for the compact variant, both clear of the floor. The risk is a future tightening of that 26px, not today's value.

3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (AA)

No cognitive test may be required without an alternative. Password paste and password managers must work; biometric app-lock is an addition, never the only route.

Affects screen 31. There is a biometric lock in the mobile engine today — it must never become the sole way in.

Questions. (1) Should I treat "Move to position…" as satisfying 2.5.7, or do you want a fuller non-drag flow? (2) Is WCAG 2.2 AA the right bar, or does a school procurement process require something else (e.g. a VPAT, or 2.1 AA)? (3) Do any of your schools have a stated accessibility requirement I should design to?
